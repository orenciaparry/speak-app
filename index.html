For grown-ups and the speech therapist

Made for Elijah, to run alongside his speech therapy at the level his therapist sets. It is not a replacement for therapy. Please tell us what is wrong or unclear so it can be fixed. This panel opens with a press-and-hold so Elijah does not wander in.

Where Elijah is right now

  • He has made a clean /s/ a few times but defaults to the tongue-between-teeth pattern. The daily job is repetitions until it stops being an effort: aim for 30 clear S sounds a day in short bursts (the target is adjustable below).
  • Two tricks for the "forcing": the long t (t‑t‑t‑tsss, the tongue is already on the ridge for a t) and the quiet S (a whisper-soft sss; pushing hard tends to slide the tongue forward). Both are under the Long S tab.
  • The Start / End / Middle / Together / Sentences tabs are built and ready, but long S comes first. The therapist decides when to move on.
  • We say "the S sound" and always model it as "sss", never the letter name "ess". Likewise "d" and "g", not "dee" and "gee".

What the picture shows

  • A side-on (midline) cutaway of the mouth: lips, front teeth, the alveolar ridge, the hard and soft palate, the tongue, and a “voice box” marker for the larynx.

Progress, last 7 days

Daily target: clear S sounds a day. Only "Yes" counts towards it.

Voice

The reading voice comes from this device's own text-to-speech. Pick one that sounds right, choose a speed, and test it. British female voices are chosen automatically when the device has one, and iPhones and iPads start on Slow because their voices run faster.

Personal words and photos

  • Sia, Elise, sisters, St. Kitts, sea, starfish, swimming, sky, school and smile all come from Elijah's own family and photos. Shiloh starts with "sh", a different sound, so it only appears as a "not an S" word in the listening game.
  • Only words spelled with the letter S are on the cards, so the orange letter always matches the sound. Words like ice, face and pencil, where a C makes the S sound, are left out on purpose. "Jesus" stays in the sentences (its final S is a true S; the middle one is a Z).
  • "Jesus", "sisters" and "Elise's" end in, or contain, a Z sound: the same tongue position with the voice on. Children with this pattern usually lisp Z the same way; the therapist may want to treat the two together.
  • In the uniform photo Shiloh is on the left and Sia on the right; in the coats photo Shiloh is in purple and Sia in pink.
  • Photos are part of the app file and are not uploaded anywhere.
